# Professional, B2B, education and community: what world-class marketing photography looks like

Read the section for the business's industry before writing the brief and shot list (SKILL.md step 3).
House style and Never go into every prompt; Shot concepts seed the shot list; Real only is never generated.

- **Benchmarks** are brands whose photography sets the bar. Brand websites were checked live on
  2026-11-20; Instagram handles were not. "(confirm)" marks a lower-confidence pick. Open the account
  before naming it to a client. Never copy a benchmark's photos: they are cited for style only.
- **Reference photos** are free-licence (Unsplash/Pexels) shots found by text search, not yet viewed.
  Open and judge one before using it as a mood reference; never attach an unviewed image as a
  `style_refs` anchor. Viewed and approved anchors live in `references/anchors/` (see manifest.json).

### Law firm
Also covers: solicitors, barristers, notaries, immigration and visa consultants, IP and patent attorneys, family-law practices, legal-tech.

**Benchmarks**
- Slaughter and May (London) — https://www.slaughterandmay.com — restraint: architecture and stone, almost no people; proves "serious" can mean quiet, not gavels.
- Mishcon de Reya (London) — https://www.mishcon.com — real lawyers photographed as individuals in their own offices with a bold, editorial crop, not boardroom smiles.
- Quinn Emanuel (Los Angeles) — https://www.quinnemanuel.com — stark, high-contrast portraits of named partners; confident rather than friendly.
- Clifford Chance (London) — https://www.cliffordchance.com — city and building photography with human scale; cool, daylight-only grade.
- Al Tamimi & Company (Dubai) — https://www.tamimi.com — Gulf reference: Dubai/Abu Dhabi skyline and office interiors with real regional professionals.
- Nishimura & Asahi (Tokyo) — https://www.nishimura.com — Asia reference: architectural, minimal, people used sparingly.
- Atrium Law Group style independents on Instagram: search `@mishcondereya` for the closest social execution of "law firm that looks like a magazine".

**House style**
- Light: soft window daylight, north-facing if possible; no flash, no window-blown highlights.
- Lens and distance: 35 to 85 mm; environmental portraits at 2 to 3 m, details (pen, paper edge, spine of a bound agreement) at 100 mm macro-ish.
- Colour and grade: muted, slightly desaturated neutrals; warm wood and paper, cool stone; no teal-orange.
- People: the actual lawyer, mid-task (reading, annotating, listening to a client), looking at the work or off-frame, never at the lens grinning.
- Props, materials, setting: bound files, fountain pens, library shelving, real conference room with real clutter; never scales of justice or gavels.
- Framing: quiet negative space; asymmetric, shoulder-height portraits; hands-and-document close-ups.

**Never**
- Scales of justice, gavel, Lady Justice statue, courthouse columns as hero image.
- Handshake over a contract, "signing" hands with a fake smile.
- Suit-and-tie model pointing at a laptop with colleagues clapping.
- Glowing blue "legal tech" overlays, padlock icons over a city.
- Blown-out white glass conference room with an empty chair set.

**Shot concepts**
- Partner annotating a draft agreement at a window desk, pen mid-stroke.
- Associate carrying a trolley of ring binders down a corridor.
- Client and lawyer across a table, lawyer listening, client mid-sentence (side-on, no lens contact).
- Stack of redlined pages with a coffee ring and a highlighter.
- Library or reference shelves with a lawyer reaching for a volume.
- Receptionist greeting a client in the entrance, daylight behind.
- Hands passing a signed document across a desk (close crop, no faces).
- Office building at dusk with a single lit floor.

**Showcase concepts** (real proof only: craft.md section 2)
- The firm's website and client portal in a browser frame (`mockup browser`), or desktop and phone together (`pair`).
- A guide, briefing or insight the firm really published, captured from its site.
- Its booking or enquiry flow on a phone (`mockup phone`).
- A ranking or award the site shows, captured as it appears there (never redrawn).
- A generated desk scene with the firm's real site composited on the laptop screen (`screen`).
- Client matters are confidential: never mock up a case file, a court document or a client name.

**Real only**
- Named lawyers and partners (headshots, team pages).
- The firm's actual offices, reception and meeting rooms.
- Any client, court or case-linked imagery; anything implying a specific matter.

### Accounting, finance and wealth management
Also covers: bookkeepers, tax advisers, auditors, independent financial advisers, family offices, mortgage brokers, insurance brokers, fintech.

**Benchmarks**
- Wealthsimple (Toronto) — https://www.wealthsimple.com — real-people portraits and lifestyle moments in homes and streets, warm and un-glossy; no coins, no charts.
- Mercury (San Francisco) — https://mercury.com — founder-led editorial photography and illustration of real business owners at work, not "bank" imagery.
- Monzo (London) — https://monzo.com — candid people in daily life and cities, deliberately un-corporate.
- Hiscox / Aviva-style retail insurers are generic; skip. Instead: Brex (San Francisco) — https://www.brex.com — founders photographed in their real workplaces.
- Wio Bank (Abu Dhabi) — https://www.wio.io — Gulf reference: contemporary, human, SME-focused imagery.
- Revolut (London) — https://www.revolut.com — fast, bold street and portrait photography at global scale.
- Endowus (Singapore) — https://endowus.com — Asia reference: clean, daylight, real clients and advisers.

**House style**
- Light: window daylight, golden-hour exterior; avoid fluorescent office cast.
- Lens and distance: 35 to 50 mm for portraits in context, 85 to 100 mm for hands and paper.
- Colour and grade: warm neutrals and true skin tones; one restrained accent colour; flat, not glossy.
- People: the client at their own kitchen table, shop counter or workshop; the adviser listening with a notebook; eyes on a task or each other.
- Props, materials, setting: paper statements, a laptop with plain screen, a shop till, a family kitchen; calculators only when the business really uses them.
- Framing: portrait-oriented, off-centre subject, room for text on one side.

**Never**
- Piggy banks, stacked coins, rising-arrow charts, glowing globes.
- Suit-and-tie handshakes or "approved" stamps.
- Couple looking at a tablet with a fake delighted laugh.
- Calculator-and-cash flat lays used as the whole identity.
- Generated skylines with blue data overlays.

**Shot concepts**
- Adviser and client at a home dining table with printed statements and tea.
- Shop owner reconciling the day's takings after closing.
- Accountant's hands highlighting a ledger line.
- Founder reading a bank notification on the shop floor, phone in frame.
- Receipts and a laptop on a kitchen counter at night, one lamp.
- Pair reviewing a mortgage offer on a sofa, papers on knees.
- Adviser walking into the office at 8 am with coffee, street behind.
- Family office desk by a window: pen, leather folder, one plant.

**Showcase concepts** (real proof only: craft.md section 2)
- The client portal, app or dashboard the firm gives clients, captured or supplied (`mockup browser` or `element`).
- A tool on its own site (a tax or fee calculator, a checklist) captured as an element.
- A report pack or monthly summary it produces, supplied by the owner and anonymised.
- A metric quoted word for word from the site, with clean ground left for it to be set as type.
- A generated home-office or shop-counter scene with the real portal composited on the screen (`screen`).

**Real only**
- Advisers, partners and staff.
- Client testimonials and case portraits.
- The firm's actual office; any real customer's business premises.

### Consulting and B2B services
Also covers: management and strategy consultants, HR and recruitment firms, PR, market research, IT services, business coaching, translation.

**Benchmarks**
- McKinsey (New York) — https://www.mckinsey.com — editorial photography commissioned for features: real places and workers, not office stock.
- BCG (Boston) — https://www.bcg.com — documentary-feeling location photography in its insights content.
- Pentagram's client work (London/New York) — https://www.pentagram.com — shows how pro services brands use art-directed stills.
- Kearney (Chicago) — https://www.kearney.com — real client environments and field shots.
- Strategy&/PwC Middle East (Dubai) — https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/m1 — Gulf reference: regional project and city photography, local professionals.
- Bain & Company (Boston) — https://www.bain.com — people shown mid-conversation in real workplaces.
- Nomura Research Institute (Tokyo) — https://www.nri.com/en — Asia reference: restrained architecture and workplace documentary.

**House style**
- Light: ambient office or site daylight; avoid ceiling-flash glare on glass.
- Lens and distance: 35 mm for rooms and groups, 85 mm for portraits; stand back from the whiteboard.
- Colour and grade: neutral and slightly cool, skin tones kept honest; one deep accent allowed.
- People: two to four real colleagues in an actual working moment, eyes on the problem or each other, not the lens.
- Props, materials, setting: marker-covered glass, printed decks, sticky notes, a client's actual shop floor or site.
- Framing: documentary wide with foreground blur; avoid centred symmetry.

**Never**
- Standing person pointing at a wall chart while others nod.
- Handshake across a glass table.
- Group of suited diverse "team" arms-crossed pose.
- Silhouette against a sunrise cityscape.
- Gears, puzzle pieces, light bulbs, arrows.

**Shot concepts**
- Two consultants arguing over a half-drawn diagram on glass.
- Consultant walking a client's factory floor with a hard hat and tablet at their side.
- Printed deck pages spread on a table with pen annotations.
- Late-afternoon workshop: sticky notes mid-sort, hands moving them.
- Interview over coffee, notebook open, one speaker blurred in foreground.
- Team at a train station platform with luggage on a project day.
- Laptop review at a shared table, two faces lit by daylight.
- Handwritten whiteboard with blurred person erasing.

**Showcase concepts** (real proof only: craft.md section 2)
- A case study or published report page in a browser frame (`mockup browser`, `--full` for portrait).
- A framework, diagram or chart from the firm's own site, captured as an element.
- A deliverable the owner supplies (a deck page, a dashboard), anonymised.
- A metric from a case study, quoted word for word, with clean ground for type.
- A generated meeting-room scene with the real report composited on a laptop or screen (`screen`).

**Real only**
- The firm's actual consultants and leadership.
- Client sites and staff; any real project output or data.
- Named quote portraits.

### SaaS and tech company
Also covers: B2B software, apps, dev tools, AI startups, IT and managed-services providers, hardware startups, data and analytics firms.

**Benchmarks**
- Mercury (San Francisco) — https://mercury.com — real customers and founders in their workplaces rather than UI stock.
- Shopify (Ottawa) — https://www.shopify.com — merchants photographed in actual shops and studios; the best "real people, real places" B2B reference.
- Squarespace (New York) — https://www.squarespace.com — creator and small-business portraits at their real premises.
- Figma (San Francisco) — https://www.figma.com — real teams in real studios in case studies, hands on keyboards, plants and clutter.
- Stripe Press (San Francisco) — https://press.stripe.com — book and object photography; very controlled, tactile still life.
- Tabby (Riyadh/Dubai) — https://tabby.ai — Gulf reference: regional consumers and merchants photographed in real malls and homes.
- Freee (Tokyo) — https://www.freee.co.jp — Asia reference: owner-operators in their own shops and offices.

**House style**
- Light: daylight from large windows; practical lamps at night for a lived-in feel.
- Lens and distance: 35 mm over-the-shoulder, 50 to 85 mm for faces; in mood slots, screens deliberately soft or turned away (showcase slots show the real UI, captured).
- Colour and grade: clean, mid-key, slightly warm; no neon "tech blue".
- People: real users and builders, doing the job, looking at the work or each other.
- Props, materials, setting: sticker-covered laptops, notebooks, cables, real desks; the product shown on a phone in a hand, not a floating mockup.
- Framing: shallow depth of field; in mood slots, screens never centre stage.

**Never**
- Floating UI mockups over a glowing laptop at a "perfect" desk.
- Developer in a hoodie in a dark room with green code rain.
- Person pointing at a screen while three others lean in.
- Team high-fiving around a laptop.
- Illustrated 3D blob people replacing a real team.

**Shot concepts**
- Founder at a late-night desk, one lamp, phone beside the laptop.
- Two engineers at one monitor, one gesturing, faces side-lit.
- Customer (a baker or electrician) using the product on a phone at their real counter.
- Whiteboard photographed after a planning session, blurry teammate behind.
- Hands unboxing a hardware device on a wooden desk.
- Standup in a corridor, coffee cups in hand.
- Commute shot: phone in hand on a tram, product on screen soft.
- Office window at dusk with one team still working.

**Showcase concepts** (real proof only: craft.md section 2)
- The product's main screen in a browser frame (`mockup browser`), captured or supplied.
- Desktop app and mobile app together (`mockup pair`).
- One feature, close: a chart, a card, a chat answer, captured with `--selector` (`mockup element`).
- The mobile app in a phone, for stories (`mockup phone`).
- A customer's counter or desk, generated, with the real UI composited on the phone or laptop screen (`screen`).
- Before and after: the old workflow (a spreadsheet the owner supplies) next to the product (`mockup split`).

**Real only**
- The real team and customers.
- The actual product UI and any metrics (capture them or ask the owner; never generate).
- The company's real office or store.

### Creative, marketing and design agency
Also covers: branding studios, ad agencies, web and app design shops, architecture and interior design studios, video and content production houses.

**Benchmarks**
- Pentagram (London/New York) — https://www.pentagram.com — photographs finished work in situ plus the partners themselves, no agency-selfie clichés.
- Collins (San Francisco) — https://www.wearecollins.com — spreads of real print, packaging and spaces; studio photographed with a documentary eye.

- Colour and grade: true-to-material; match the studio's palette; low contrast, no HDR.
- People: designers mid-craft, hands and tools in focus, face secondary.
- Props, materials, setting: pinboards, swatches, print proofs, Pantone chips, models, cutting mats.

**Never**
- "Creative team" jumping or laughing in a bean-bag room.
- Brainstorming sticky-note wall posed with markers raised.

- Fake hand-drawn sketch over a stock photo.

- Designer pinning a print proof next to others on a studio wall.
- Hands swapping Pantone chips on a palette, close crop.
- Two designers critiquing a physical packaging mock-up.
- Overhead of a messy desk mid-project: pencils, loupe, laptop.
- Model-maker cutting foam board under a lamp.
- Studio kitchen: team lunch with leftover work on the table.
- Printer-room shot: wet proofs hung to dry.

- A client site the agency built, in a browser frame or desktop and phone together (`mockup browser` / `pair`).
- Before and after of a redesign, from the Wayback Machine or the agency's own case study (`mockup split`).
- A case-study page or portfolio grid captured from the agency's site.
- Campaign creative it made, supplied by the owner (real pixels, so the creative's own text is fine).
- A generated studio desk with a real client site composited on the laptop screen (`screen`).

**Real only**
- The studio team and founders.
- The agency's actual client work and clients.
- The actual studio space.

### Education, tutoring and schools
Also covers: language schools, music and art classes, universities, nurseries (see Childcare), driving schools, coding bootcamps, test-prep centres.

**Benchmarks**
- High Tech High (San Diego) — https://www.hightechhigh.org — real student project work and honest classroom candids.
- Green School (Bali) — https://www.greenschool.org/bali — Asia reference: bamboo campus photographed in daylight, children unposed.
- NYU Abu Dhabi — https://nyuad.nyu.edu — Gulf reference: campus architecture and real students in documentary light.
- Reggio Children (Reggio Emilia) — https://www.reggiochildren.it — child-led work photographed at child height.
- MasterClass (San Francisco) — https://www.masterclass.com — instructors in their own environment, cinematic.
- Eton College (UK) — https://www.etoncollege.com — uniform, tradition and architecture without cliché poses.
- Singapore American School — https://www.sas.edu.sg — documentary student life and outdoors.

**House style**
- Light: daylight from classroom windows; no flash on children.
- Lens and distance: 35 to 50 mm at child eye-line; 85 mm for hands.
- Colour and grade: natural and warm; school colours appear organically in the scene.
- People: a teacher crouched at a desk, a student mid-problem, mid-explanation; faces absorbed, not performing.
- Props, materials, setting: real classroom clutter, worn desks, pencils, board work, lab benches.
- Framing: eye-level documentary; leave foreground clutter in.

**Never**
- Rows of kids with raised hands and open mouths.
- Child pointing at a globe, or at a laptop with a teacher beaming.
- Apple-on-the-desk, graduation cap toss, chalkboard formula overlays.
- Staged "multicultural" line-up.
- Children's faces generated or composited.

**Shot concepts**
