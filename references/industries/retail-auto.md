# Retail, products and auto: what world-class marketing photography looks like

Read the section for the business's industry before writing the brief and shot list (SKILL.md step 3).
House style and Never go into every prompt; Shot concepts seed the shot list; Real only is never generated.

- **Benchmarks** are brands whose photography sets the bar. Brand websites were checked live on
  2026-11-20; Instagram handles were not. "(confirm)" marks a lower-confidence pick. Open the account
  before naming it to a client. Never copy a benchmark's photos: they are cited for style only.
- **Reference photos** are free-licence (Unsplash/Pexels) shots found by text search, not yet viewed.
  Open and judge one before using it as a mood reference; never attach an unviewed image as a
  `style_refs` anchor. Viewed and approved anchors live in `references/anchors/` (see manifest.json).

### Fashion and apparel boutique
Also covers: concept store, menswear/womenswear label, streetwear shop, vintage and resale shop, tailor, modest-wear and abaya label, kidswear, shoe boutique

**Benchmarks**
- Jacquemus (Paris) — https://www.instagram.com/jacquemus/ — campaigns shot in real places (lavender fields, salt flats, Marseille streets) with hard sun and one idea per frame, not catalogue grids.
- Lemaire (Paris) — https://www.lemaire.fr — quiet, soft overcast light, relaxed real bodies in motion, fabric drape shown rather than posed fit.
- Toteme (Stockholm) — https://toteme-studio.com — restrained neutral palette, architectural interiors, garment detail and fabric texture treated as the hero.
- Auralee (Tokyo) — https://auralee.jp — still, muted, texture-first imagery; shows what cloth feels like.
- QASIMI (Dubai/Sharjah) — https://qasimi.com (unconfirmed; SS23 campaign shot in Sharjah on film by Tariq Al Hajri) — Gulf-rooted, film grain, local architecture and light instead of a generic Dubai skyline.
- Bouguessa (Dubai) — https://www.instagram.com/bouguessa/ (unconfirmed) — Gulf designer label with editorial rather than e-commerce framing.
- Clark Street Mercantile (independent boutique) — https://www.instagram.com/clarkstreetmercantile/ (unconfirmed) — shows a real shop with real stock; the kind of independent place whose interiors are worth emulating.

**House style**
- Light: soft window daylight or hard low sun; shade side of a doorway; no on-camera flash unless it is a deliberate flash editorial.
- Lens and distance: 35mm and 50mm for rails and rooms, 85mm for fabric and collar details; stand close enough to see weave.
- Colour and grade: natural, slightly desaturated, warm shadows; film-like grain is fine, teal-orange and HDR are not.
- People: real staff or a model mid-action (adjusting a cuff, crossing a street, looking away), eyes rarely on lens, no big smile.
- Props, materials, setting: the actual shop, rail, fitting room curtain, tissue and garment bag, steamer, hangers, a worn wood floor; garment creases left in.
- Framing: asymmetric, off-centre, tight crops on a sleeve or hem, a rail receding in depth; cut people at the shoulder or knee.

**Never**
- Ghost-mannequin or white-seamless catalogue shots passed off as brand imagery.
- Model with a wide toothy smile staring at camera in front of a blurred "boutique".
- Perfectly pressed, symmetrical rails with every hanger evenly spaced and identical colour fade.
- Shopping bags held up to the lens, or a woman laughing at a phone while shopping.
- AI fabric that has no weave, wrong buttons or stitching, or hands with extra fingers on a garment.

**Shot concepts**
- Steamer held to a linen shirt on the rail, steam backlit by the window.
- Hand pulling a single hanger from a crowded rail, sleeve in sharp focus.
- Customer seen from behind in the fitting room mirror, curtain half drawn.
- Tailor chalking a hem with pins in the mouth, tape around the neck.
- Garment folded on a worn oak table, tissue paper and label visible.
- Owner opening the shutters at 9am, first light across the floor.
- Close-up of buttons and stitching on a new-season coat.
- Wrapping a purchase in tissue and tying it with string at the counter.

**Real only**
- The actual shop interior and exterior, the owner and staff, the real stock and its labels, and real customers (with consent). Generated clothes are acceptable only as clearly labelled concept mock-ups.

### Jewellery and watches
Also covers: bespoke jeweller, bridal and engagement ring maker, watch dealer, vintage watch restorer, pawn and luxury resale, gold souk trader, goldsmith

**Benchmarks**
- Mejuri (Toronto) — https://mejuri.com — jewellery worn on real skin in daylight, ear and hand close-ups, everyday not gala.
- Catbird (Brooklyn) — https://www.catbirdnyc.com — real customers, hands and stacked rings, Brooklyn light, film warmth.
- Monica Vinader (London) — https://www.monicavinader.com — soft natural light on skin, close macro of stones, women of varied ages.
- Hodinkee (New York) — https://www.hodinkee.com — the benchmark for watch photography: honest macro, wrist shots in real settings, patina and scratches shown.
- Grand Seiko (Tokyo) — https://www.grand-seiko.com — landscape-linked dial photography, light grazing across Zaratsu polish.
- Azza Fahmy (Cairo/Dubai) — https://azzafahmy.com — Gulf and Egyptian craft jewellery, artisan hands and heritage motifs.
- Tasaki (Tokyo) — https://www.tasaki.co.jp — pearls and gold shot with restraint on stone and skin.

**House style**
- Light: single large window or open shade for skin; soft side light with a dark card for gold depth; a small hard point of light only to make a stone sparkle.
- Lens and distance: 100mm macro for stones and dials, 50-85mm for hands and wrists; shallow depth of field but the nearest edge sharp.
- Colour and grade: true metal colour (gold stays gold, not orange), neutral skin, deep not crushed blacks; no blown highlights on polish.
- People: hands, wrists, neck and ear crops of real people; a jeweller at the bench with loupe or saw; face usually out of frame or turned away.
- Props, materials, setting: bench pegs, saws, files, polishing mops, pickle pot, linen, plaster, stone, travertine, worn leather, tools in use.
- Framing: tight crops, negative space on one side, wrist across diagonal; watch shown at an angle with case flank, not flat 10:10 only.

**Never**
- Black velvet bust with a spotlit necklace, sparkle star filters or lens-flare stars added in post.
- Generated jewellery with impossible clasps, uneven stone settings or watch dials with unreadable fake text.
- Model with hand-on-chin pose and perfect manicure staring at the lens.
- Pure white cutouts as brand imagery, or heavily HDR watch shots with dust-white highlights.
- Gold-coin and money-pile clichés for a goldsmith.

**Shot concepts**
- Jeweller's hands soldering a ring at the bench, flame and glow in frame.
- Loupe held to a diamond under a window.
- Wax carving or filing of a ring blank, shavings on the leather.
- Customer's hand trying on a ring, jeweller's hand adjusting the size beside it.
- Watchmaker with a screwdriver on an open movement under a lamp.
- Strap swap at the counter, tools laid on a mat.
- Vintage watch on a wrist in a street, scratched case visible.
- Boxed purchase on the counter with tissue and ribbon, hands passing it over.

**Real only**
- The actual pieces for sale (stone, setting and engraving must match what ships), the maker's hands and bench, hallmarks, and any customer wearing the product. Never generate the product itself.

### Furniture and homeware store
Also covers: interior design studio shop, lighting shop, rug and textile dealer, ceramics and tableware shop, kitchenware, mattress and bed brand, outdoor furniture, souk/antique dealer

**Benchmarks**
- HAY (Copenhagen) — https://www.hay.com — real rooms with life in them, daylight, colour used as a deliberate system.
- Serax (Antwerp) — https://www.serax.com — tableware and objects staged in lived-in interiors with natural shadow.
- Karimoku (Japan) — https://www.karimoku.com — wood grain and joinery photographed with patience in plain rooms.
- Heath Ceramics (Sausalito) — https://www.heathceramics.com — makers and kiln shots alongside tabletop use; craft and use together.
- Parachute (Los Angeles) — https://www.parachutehome.com — soft morning light, rumpled linen, rooms that look slept in.
- Nada Debs (Dubai/Beirut) — https://www.nadadebs.com — Gulf design house, craft-led furniture with regional inlay and warm interiors.
- Muji (Tokyo) — https://www.muji.com — calm everyday homes, honest light, no styling theatre.

**House style**
- Light: big window daylight, side-lit so grain, weave and shadow define form; late afternoon sun patches across floors.
- Lens and distance: 24-35mm for rooms with straight verticals; 50mm for vignettes; 85mm for material detail.
- Colour and grade: warm neutrals, true wood tone, lifted but not milky shadows; no oversaturation.
- People: a hand on a chair back, someone reading on the sofa, a maker sanding; partial figures, seldom faces.
- Props, materials, setting: a real home or the showroom with its imperfections; books, a half-drunk coffee, a throw, tools on the bench.
- Framing: wide with depth and foreground layering, then tight detail pairs; keep furniture at real scale in the room.

**Never**
- Empty, perfectly symmetrical rooms with sofa, plant and fake book stack at every angle.
- Floating furniture on white with soft fake shadows when it is meant to feel like a home.
- Generated rooms with wrong chair legs, melted joinery or inconsistent window light.
- Family sitting on a sofa laughing at a TV.
- Over-bright HDR interiors with blown windows.

**Shot concepts**
- Delivery team carrying a sofa through a doorway.
- Hand running across oak table grain in raking light.
- Maker sanding a chair leg, dust in the light.
- Dining table set mid-meal, half-finished glasses.
- Rug unrolled on the showroom floor, customer crouched to feel the pile.
- Lamp switched on at dusk in a lived-in corner.
- Fabric swatch book on a customer's knee beside a window.
- Showroom opening, owner rearranging chairs before doors open.

**Real only**
- The actual products and finishes for sale, the showroom, dimensions-critical scale shots, delivered customer homes (with consent), and craftspeople. Generated rooms only as mood boards.

### DTC and e-commerce consumer products (CPG, packaged goods)
Also covers: food and drink brands, skincare and beauty, candles and fragrance, supplements, pet products, small appliances, stationery, snack and coffee brands

**Benchmarks**
- Graza (New York) — https://www.graza.co — squeeze bottle shot in real kitchens, motion and drizzle, bold flat colour.
- Fishwife (US) — https://eatfishwife.com — tinned fish as design object plus real meals, flash and daylight mix.
- Brightland (Los Angeles) — https://brightland.co — product in architectural light, sun-through-bottle shadow.
- Aesop (Melbourne) — https://www.aesop.com — amber bottles in considered, tactile rooms, dark materials.
- Our Place (Los Angeles) — https://fromourplace.com — product in use in real homes, steam, mess and people cooking.
- Mirzam Chocolate (Dubai) — https://www.mirzamchocolate.com — Gulf craft chocolate with spice and heritage cues, warm interiors.
- Ippodo Tea (Kyoto) — https://global.ippodo-tea.co.jp — tea caddies, steam, hands, quiet tabletop set-ups.

**House style**
- Light: window light shaped with a flag and bounce card; hard sun through glass or leaf for shadow pattern; one coloured gel only when brand-owned.
- Lens and distance: 50-100mm macro for packs, 35mm in-context; shoot slightly above or at eye level to the product.
- Colour and grade: the brand colour stays accurate; clean mid-tones with a single accent; avoid plasticky gloss.
- People: hands pouring, spreading, opening, tearing; a person mid-meal, face optional.
- Props, materials, setting: real kitchen, bathroom shelf, desk; crumbs, drips, wet rings, half-used products; surfaces such as stone, linen, ply.
- Framing: pack at roughly two thirds, active context around it; vary from macro to wide so a feed has rhythm.

**Never**
- Pack floating on a gradient with random fruit and splash.
- Condensation, droplets and steam that are obviously generated or uniformly sprayed.
- Wrong label text, warped logo or lettering on generated packaging.
- Hand-holding-product-to-camera with a big grin.
- Full white cutout as the only look for a lifestyle brand.

**Shot concepts**
- Olive oil drizzled over a tomato salad, motion in the stream.
- Hand tearing open a snack bag on a desk at 3pm.
- Serum dropper over the palm in bathroom window light.
- Coffee bag beside a pour-over kettle, steam rising.
- Pantry shelf with pack mid-stack and one pack half used.
- Unboxing a subscription on a doorstep step, tape and paper kept.
- Pet bowl being filled, pet's nose in frame.
- Candle lit on a dinner table at dusk, wax pool visible.

**Real only**
- Pack, label, ingredients, claims and regulatory copy; the actual product contents; any texture or result claim (skin, taste, colour). Generated imagery can only set the scene around a real pack photo.

### Florist
Also covers: flower market stall, event and wedding florist, plant shop, bouquet subscription, dried-flower studio, garden centre

**Benchmarks**
- Flowerbx (London) — https://www.flowerbx.com — close crops of whole stems, seasonal colour, moody and daylit boxes.
- McQueens Flowers (London) — https://www.mcqueens.co.uk — dramatic, painterly arrangements shot in low light like still-life paintings.
- Ode à la Rose (Paris) — https://www.odealarose.com — loose French market bouquets, delivery bicycle and street setting.
- Aoyama Flower Market (Tokyo) — https://www.aoyamaflowermarket.com — greenhouse-cafe shop interior, bouquets shown in real use.
- Gosha (Dubai) — https://www.instagram.com/gosha.flowers/ (unconfirmed handle) — Dubai florist with department-store window collaborations.
- Fine Blooms (Dubai) — https://www.instagram.com/fineblooms/ (unconfirmed) — boutique florist inside a designed retail space.
- Floret (Washington State) — https://www.floretflowers.com — farm-to-vase, rows of real blooms and the cutters.

**House style**
- Light: window or open shade, never midday overhead sun on petals; side or back light to show petal translucency; moody single window for dark florals.
- Lens and distance: 50mm and 85mm, bouquet shot at eye level to see layering; 100mm macro for texture.
- Colour and grade: true petal colour, slightly low saturation, deep greens; protect red channel from clipping.
- People: hands cutting, wrapping, tying; a florist in apron at the bucket wall; buyer carrying paper-wrapped stems.
- Props, materials, setting: buckets, kraft paper, twine, snips, stem trimmings and leaves on the bench, wet zinc, brass, marble, a messy workbench.
- Framing: asymmetric, fall-off at the edges of an arrangement; bouquet cropped to feel generous; walking shots from behind.

**Never**
- Perfect symmetrical round bouquet on white with fake dew.
- Roses-and-baby's-breath cliché in a heart shape, or red rose with heart bokeh.
- Generated flowers with impossible petal counts, merging stems or mixed-season stems that do not exist together.
- Smiling person sniffing flowers while looking at the camera.
- Floating bouquet with a hand missing.

**Shot concepts**
- Florist stripping thorns and leaves at the bench.
- Hand wrapping a bouquet in kraft paper and tying twine.
- Bucket wall at 6am after the market run, wet stems.
- Customer walking out with paper-wrapped stems, shot from behind.
- Hands arranging stems into a vase, scissors in the other hand.
- Delivery bike or van with boxes on the doorstep.
- Dried flowers hanging from a beam in low light.
- Shop shutters up, buckets set outside to the pavement.

**Real only**
- The actual bouquet being sold (real stem mix, size and wrap), the florist and shop front, same-day delivery proof, and wedding or event arrangements delivered.

### Car dealership
Also covers: used-car lot, luxury and exotic dealer, EV showroom, motorcycle dealer, car rental, classic-car dealer, car broker and importer

**Benchmarks**
- Porsche (Stuttgart) — https://www.instagram.com/porsche/ — cars photographed in real roads and weather with rolling shots, long lenses.
- Polestar (Gothenburg) — https://www.polestar.com — spare, architectural, low-contrast light, clean spaces.
- Classic Driver (Hamburg) — https://www.classicdriver.com — the benchmark for dealer listings: honest detail, patina, interior and engine bay.
- Genesis (Seoul) — https://www.genesis.com — cinematic dusk and showroom light with warm interiors.
- Bring a Trailer (US) — https://bringatrailer.com — plain, thorough, trust-building coverage of every panel and flaw.
- Lamborghini Dubai / Al Tayer Motors (Dubai) — https://www.instagram.com/lamborghini_dubai/ (unconfirmed) — Gulf showroom dealer social; use as location reference.
- Nissan NISMO Omori Factory (Yokohama) — https://www.nismo.com — workshop and showroom imagery in Japanese garage culture.

**House style**
- Light: golden hour or overcast for exteriors, dusk blue-hour with lights on; showroom cars lit with big soft overheads and reflections controlled.
- Lens and distance: 35mm for stance and interiors, 70-200mm for rolling and compressed side profiles; low three-quarter front is the standard.
- Colour and grade: accurate paint colour, strong contrast on body lines, no oversaturation of reds; keep reflections believable.
- People: customer taking a key, a hand on the door, a salesperson walking the car with them, no big-grin handshake.
- Props, materials, setting: real road, clean forecourt, workshop doors; a wet tarmac in the evening; plain showroom floor.
- Framing: car at 3/4, low; interiors with the wheel and dash as the leading line; crop to detail of badges, wheels, stitch.

**Never**
- Salesman and buyer handshake in front of a showroom car.
- Over-saturated, HDR forecourt with overlaid price starbursts as the main image.
- Generated cars with wrong wheel patterns, incorrect badges or morphing panel gaps.
- Car parked in a blank grey studio with fake reflections for a used-car listing.
- Wide-angle fisheye distortion of the car body.

**Shot concepts**
- Key hand-over at dusk, showroom glass behind.
- Low three-quarter of the car at golden hour on the forecourt.
- Detail of leather stitching and dash on a used-car listing.
- Customer's hand on the open door, seat in view.
- Showroom floor after closing, cars lit, floor reflections.
- Rolling shot on a quiet road for the hero car.
- Delivery day: new owner leaving, car pulling away.
- Engine bay and underside inspection photos for a used listing.

**Real only**
- The exact car for sale (VIN-matched paint, damage, mileage, options), the actual showroom and team, and the handover. Never generate or retouch condition, wear or damage.

### Auto repair, tyres and detailing
