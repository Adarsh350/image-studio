# Home, property and trades: what world-class marketing photography looks like

Read the section for the business's industry before writing the brief and shot list (SKILL.md step 3).
House style and Never go into every prompt; Shot concepts seed the shot list; Real only is never generated.

- **Benchmarks** are brands whose photography sets the bar. Brand websites were checked live on
  2026-11-20; Instagram handles were not. "(confirm)" marks a lower-confidence pick. Open the account
  before naming it to a client. Never copy a benchmark's photos: they are cited for style only.
- **Reference photos** are free-licence (Unsplash/Pexels) shots found by text search, not yet viewed.
  Open and judge one before using it as a mood reference; never attach an unviewed image as a
  `style_refs` anchor. Viewed and approved anchors live in `references/anchors/` (see manifest.json).

### Residential and luxury real estate
Also covers: property developers, estate agents and brokers, short-let and villa rental operators, serviced apartments, property photographers, new-build sales galleries.

**Benchmarks**
- Sotheby's International Realty (global) — https://www.sothebysrealty.com — listings shot in dusk or soft daylight with the architecture as hero and lifestyle detail (a half-drawn curtain, set table) instead of wide-angle empty rooms.
- The Agency (Beverly Hills/LA) — https://www.theagencyre.com / @theagencyre — editorial exteriors and twilight shots with lived-in styling; reads like a magazine spread, not an MLS upload.
- Ellington Properties (Dubai) — https://www.ellingtongroup.com / @ellingtonproperties — design-led developer; interiors photographed as boutique-hotel spaces, restrained palette, real materials.
- Omniyat (Dubai) — https://www.omniyat.com / @omniyat — luxury towers shot from low, dusk angles with the skyline as context rather than generic render glow.
- Aman Residences (global) — https://www.aman.com — sparse, shadow-led interiors and landscape; proves "luxury" is negative space, not gold fixtures.
- Knight Frank (London, Asia-Pacific arms) — https://www.knightfrank.com — consistent natural-light listing standard and credit-quality photography on prime stock.
- Airbnb Luxe / Plum Guide (global) — https://www.plumguide.com — homes shot to prove a feeling (morning light, a book on the bed), not a feature list.

**House style**
- Light: natural daylight only; windows balanced by exposure blending or a single bounced fill, never HDR halos. Twilight exteriors shot in the 15-minute blue window with interior lamps on. Golden-hour for exteriors in Gulf/Asia heat; avoid noon glare.
- Lens and distance: 24mm tilt-shift or 35mm at eye/chest height (about 1.2m), verticals dead straight. Detail shots at 85mm. Never ultra-wide fisheye distortion.
- Colour and grade: neutral, true-white walls, warm-leaning but not orange; muted greens; lifted but not crushed shadows.
- People: usually none. If present, one resident from behind or in profile mid-action (pouring coffee, walking to the terrace), never looking at the lens, never a couple holding a key.
- Props, materials, setting: three to five styled props max: linen throw, a book, fresh stems, one ceramic. Real stone, oak, brass. Staged but looks lived-in.
- Framing: one-point or two-point perspective, symmetrical hero shot per space, followed by a detail and a view-out. Leave sky and negative space for copy.

**Never**
- Fisheye wide-angle rooms with warped walls and blown-out windows.
- Sky replacement with fake saturated blue and glowing "dusk" composites.
- Agent holding keys or pointing at a house; couple jumping with "SOLD" signs.
- Empty, over-clean rooms with no furniture scale; 3D renders passed off as photos.
- Identical fake-fireplace-TV staging, plastic plants, stock "luxury" gold-and-marble clichés.

**Shot concepts**
- Dusk exterior of the villa with interior lamps glowing and pool reflecting.
- Morning light through a sheer curtain across a bed, linen creased.
- Kitchen island with coffee mid-pour, resident only in frame as hands and forearm.
- Hallway looking toward a terrace, door ajar, plants at the threshold.
- Marble or stone bathroom detail with towel and single stem.
- View from the balcony over the skyline, resident's back in frame.
- Entry detail: door handle, key on a dish, shoes by the door.
- Aerial of the plot or building in context at golden hour.

**Real only**
- The actual property (exterior, interior, finishes, view); never AI-generate a listing room.
- The agent/broker headshot and team photos.
- Neighbourhood and building-in-context shots.
- Anything implying a specific unit, floorplan view or sold status.

### Interior design and architecture studio
Also covers: architects, interior architects, furniture/lighting showrooms, kitchen and bath designers, home stagers, design-build firms.

**Benchmarks**
- Studio McGee (Utah) — https://studio-mcgee.com / @studiomcgee — bright, layered, shot-for-the-feed rooms with consistent styling that makes the whole portfolio recognisable.
- Rose Uniacke (London) — https://www.roseuniacke.com — quiet, tonal rooms photographed in soft window light; texture and proportion do the work.
- Axel Vervoordt (Belgium) — https://www.axel-vervoordt.com — wabi-sabi interiors shot with deep shadow and patina; the opposite of glossy.
- Neri&Hu (Shanghai) — https://www.neriandhu.com — architecture photographed as atmosphere: layered light, material contrast, tiny human presence.
- Vincent Van Duysen (Antwerp) — https://www.vincentvanduysen.com — monochrome, monolithic rooms with extreme restraint; light is the subject.
- Bishop Design (Dubai) — https://www.bishopdesign.ae — Gulf residential/hospitality work photographed with warm natural light and material close-ups rather than showroom-straight.
- Studio Mumbai (India) — https://www.studiomumbai.com — handcrafted, weathered buildings in real light, with the craft process visible.

**House style**
- Light: daylight only, single dominant window source, soft falloff into shadow; raking late-afternoon light across texture. No flash, no ceiling-light blast.
- Lens and distance: 24-35mm tilt-shift for full rooms, verticals corrected; 50-85mm for vignettes and material studies. Camera at chest height, slightly low to show ceiling proportions.
- Colour and grade: restrained, tonal palettes (stone, oat, clay, olive); film-like softness, slightly lifted blacks; no saturation boost.
- People: rare. When present, a resident or craftsperson, partial, mid-task, blurred or from behind.
- Props, materials, setting: real lime plaster, travertine, oak, linen, ceramics, a half-open book; imperfect surfaces. Rooms look inhabited, not unboxed.
- Framing: strong geometry, symmetry or deliberate asymmetry, doorway-through-doorway layering, vertical crops for feed, detail-to-wide sequences.

**Never**
- Showroom-bright, flat, evenly lit rooms with every lamp on.
- Rooms with floating or impossible furniture, melted chair legs, mirror/window logic errors (common AI tells).
- Fake "luxury" gold/marble overload and symmetrical staged candles.
- Fisheye or heavily distorted wide-angle.
- Mood-board collage backgrounds and lifestyle stock couples on sofas.

**Shot concepts**
- Window light moving across a plaster wall and a ceramic on a console.
- Material board on a worktable: fabric swatches, stone samples, pencil.
- Designer's hands sketching or editing an elevation at a drafting table.
- Doorway-to-doorway view through three rooms with a pool of light at the end.
- Architectural model on a studio table with a scale figure.
- Finished bedroom corner at golden hour: linen, wooden stool, a book.
- Detail of a joinery junction, brass handle or edge profile.
- Studio team reviewing a drawing at a long table, candid, no eye contact.

**Real only**
- Completed client projects and their actual finishes.
- The designer/architect portrait and studio.
- Construction-site progress and model photography.
- Any shot claiming to be a specific delivered project.

### Home services (HVAC, plumbing, electrical)
Also covers: handyman, appliance repair, pest control, locksmith, pool maintenance, smart-home installers, AC servicing, gas fitters.

**Benchmarks**
- Quilt (US heat pump) — https://www.quilt.com — design-led HVAC; product and homes photographed like furniture, in calm daylight, never in a basement.
- Octopus Energy (UK) — https://octopus.energy — heat pump and install imagery uses real engineers in real homes, natural light, with genuine candid behaviour.
- Urban Company (India, UAE, Singapore) — https://www.urbancompany.com — consistent real-pro shots in customers' homes; clean, uniformed, mid-task.
- Justlife (Dubai) — https://justlife.com — Gulf home-services leader; real professionals and customer homes rather than white-background stock.
- Dyson (global) — https://www.dyson.com — HVAC/air products shot as engineered objects with macro detail; useful for product-surface work.
- Hippo / Neighborly local-pro brands — https://www.neighborly.com — best examples shoot a named local technician on a real job (use as a caution reference rather than a gold standard).
- Local independents: search "heat pump installer" or "master plumber" Instagram for craft-forward small firms; their edge is the same real-tech-on-real-job approach.

**House style**
- Light: available daylight from a window/open door, or a single practical work lamp; no on-camera flash. Under-sink and loft shots use a handheld LED spill, kept warm.
- Lens and distance: 35mm and 50mm at working distance; macro-ish 85mm on hands and fittings. Shoot over the shoulder so the viewer sees the work, not the camera.
- Colour and grade: true-to-life colour, muted, slightly warm, no teal-orange; keep metal looking like metal (copper, brass, stainless) and uniforms clean but not catalogue-new.
- People: one real tradesperson, in profile or three-quarter, eyes on the work, hands doing the job. Customer, if shown, is a relaxed background presence, not a smiling thumbs-up.
- Props, materials, setting: real fittings, copper pipe, torque wrench, multimeter, consumer-unit, outdoor AC condenser; a tidy van or dust sheet on the floor. Real kitchens and utility rooms.
- Framing: tight on hands and tool; or medium shot with the home visible; clean negative space for copy; vertical for stories.

**Never**
- Plumber in perfect coveralls grinning at the lens with a wrench held up.
- Fake gleaming white-set "bathroom" with pristine tools and no mess.
- AI-generated pipes that connect to nothing, extra fingers, impossible fittings.
- Pointing at a boiler or tablet; handshake at the door.
- Hard hats on a domestic electrician; hazard-icon clip-art overlays.

**Shot concepts**
- Hands tightening a compression fitting under a kitchen sink, work lamp spill on the pipes.
- Electrician testing a socket with a multimeter, consumer unit open behind.
- Technician cleaning an outdoor AC condenser coil at golden hour.
- Engineer on a stepladder fitting a ceiling unit, sunlight from the window.
- Close-up of a copper pipe being soldered, flux and flame glow.
- Customer's hand turning a thermostat dial after the install, evening light.
- Van doors open with organised tool racks and a boot-covered shoe at the porch.
- Smart thermostat on a wall with a hand adjusting it, house-lived-in context.

**Real only**
- The actual technicians, vans and uniforms.
- Real installation jobs and before/after states.
- Licence/certification marks, safety equipment, regulated work.
- Customer homes and customer faces (consent).

### Cleaning services
Also covers: domestic and commercial cleaners, laundry and dry-cleaning, window cleaners, carpet and upholstery cleaning, car detailing, deep-clean and move-out cleaners.

**Benchmarks**
- Justlife (Dubai) — https://justlife.com — real cleaners, real homes, soft daylight, practical rather than staged.
- Urban Company (India, UAE, Singapore) — https://www.urbancompany.com — clean, uniformed professionals mid-task in believable homes.
- Blueland (US) — https://www.blueland.com — product-first cleaning shot on warm daylight surfaces; shows how to make cleaning look desirable.
- Branch Basics (US) — https://branchbasics.com — real-home, soft-light lifestyle; hands, steam, textured cloths.
- Mrs. Meyer's / Grove Collaborative (US) — https://www.grove.co — seasonal, colourful product-surface still life.
- Method (US) — https://methodhome.com — design-forward bottles and bold, graphic staging; a model for premium visual language.
- Note: pure cleaning-service companies are generally weak on photography; the above include product brands as the realistic bar. Independent local firms to look for: boutique "clean + home care" studios on Instagram.

**House style**
- Light: bright diffuse window light, early-morning or late-afternoon, with sun streaks across surfaces; avoid flash gloss.
- Lens and distance: 35-50mm medium and 85mm macro for cloth, spray and wiped surface; low angle on a gleaming floor.
- Colour and grade: clean whites and light woods with one accent colour; true whites (not blue); no neon-blue sparkle effect.
- People: professional mid-task, eyes down, partial face or hands; focus on motion (wipe, polish, fold).
- Props, materials, setting: microfibre cloths, glass with real fingerprints being wiped, steam, a mop, folded linen; lived-in homes with believable light mess.
- Framing: tight on hands and surface; or mid-wide of an entire room in transformation; strong horizontals; negative space.

**Never**
- Smiling woman in rubber gloves holding a spray bottle to camera.
- Sparkle/starburst effects, blue-tinted "clean" surfaces.
- Pristine, empty sterile rooms with no sign of life.
- AI hands with merged fingers on a cloth; cloth that doesn't touch the surface.
- Rainbow of chemical bottles lined up like a pharmacy.

**Shot concepts**
- A cloth wiping a fingerprinted window with a visible clean stripe in sunlight.
- Cleaner folding fresh bedsheets on a bed, linen texture in raking light.
- Hands tying an apron by a kitchen counter, tools laid out in order.
- Mopping a wood floor, light reflecting in the wet streak.
- Reflection in a freshly polished glass or mirror with a person partially visible.
- Before/after of a stairwell or kitchen counter, same angle and light.
- Cleaner packing a caddy at the doorstep, early morning.
- Close-up of a hand wiping a stainless sink, water beads catching light.

**Real only**
- The real cleaning team, uniforms and kit.
- Real before/after of an actual client job (with consent).
- Any certification, insurance or eco-product claim visuals.

### Landscaping and garden design
Also covers: garden centres, nurseries, tree surgeons, lawn care, outdoor living and pool surrounds, irrigation, hardscape and paving, florists-with-gardens.

**Benchmarks**
- Piet Oudolf (Netherlands) — https://oudolf.com — naturalistic perennial planting photographed in late-season low light with seedheads, mist and backlight.
- Tom Stuart-Smith (UK) — https://www.tomstuartsmith.co.uk — garden structure and planting shot through seasons; layered, painterly, human scale.
- Terremoto (Los Angeles) — https://www.terremoto.la — modern, sculptural, arid gardens in slightly low, textural light.

- Dusty Gedge / Middle East: the Gulf's strongest names (e.g. desert-landscape studios in Dubai/Abu Dhabi) are not confirmed in my search; verify locally before citing a Gulf benchmark.
- Great Dixter / Sissinghurst (UK) — https://www.greatdixter.co.uk — garden-as-place photography; lived-in, seasonal.

**House style**
- Light: golden hour and overcast softness; backlight through grasses and seedheads; early-morning mist. Avoid flat noon sun and overexposed lawns.
- Lens and distance: 35-50mm at eye height for garden paths; 85-200mm compression for layered planting; macro for blooms and soil texture. Low angles through foliage.
- Colour and grade: natural greens kept varied (not neon), warm highlights, preserved blossom and bark colour; no HDR sheen; film-like softness.
- People: gardener or designer seen from behind or in profile, hands in soil, kneeling or walking a path; clothing muted; no staring at camera.
- Props, materials, setting: real soil, mulch, stone, timber; tools on a garden wall; a wheelbarrow, teacups on a bench; weathered containers.
- Framing: paths leading in, layered planes, doorways and gates, overhead plan-like shots, tight texture studies.

**Never**
- Lawn photographed at noon with uniform green and a plastic sprinkler.
- Saturated "rainbow" flowerbeds and HDR foliage.
- AI gardens with impossible plant junctions, fake flower mush, melted statues.
- Smiling gardener holding a shovel or flowers to camera.
- Lone potted plant on a white background standing in for a garden service.

**Shot concepts**
- Hands planting a perennial plug into a prepared bed, soil on gloves.
- Backlit grasses along a gravel path at golden hour.
- Designer marking out a bed with string and pegs at dawn.
- Stone path winding into a layered border with a distant bench.
- Gardener's boots and trowel on a freshly turned bed.
- Pool surround with planted terrace and loungers at dusk.
- Hand pruning a shrub, secateurs and cuttings in frame.
- Overhead of a newly installed courtyard garden, plan-like geometry.

**Real only**
- Completed client gardens and the actual plant palette.
- The designer/crew portraits and nursery stock.
- Seasonal growth before/after sequences.
- Any plant-health, tree-surgery or irrigation work claim.

### Construction, renovation and builders
Also covers: general contractors, kitchen and bathroom remodellers, joiners and carpenters, roofers, painters and decorators, fit-out firms, flooring and tile installers, pool builders.

**Benchmarks**
- Matriarchy Build (New York) — https://www.matriarchybuild.com / @matriarchybuild — woman-led plumbing/build firm; real job-site photography, strong brand voice, hands and tools.
- Hammer & Hand (Portland) — https://www.hammerandhand.com — craft-forward builders; photographs the process (framing, insulation) as carefully as the finished home.
- Heartwood Builders / Lyons Builders (US/UK style) — https://www.heartwoodbuilders.com — project and craft documentation shot in daylight, no hero-lens staging.
- Reform (Copenhagen) — https://reformcph.com — kitchen fronts photographed as architecture-quality still life in natural light.
- Studio Mumbai (India) — https://www.studiomumbai.com — builders-as-craftspeople; process and workmanship are the visual.
- Gulf and Asia: verify locally; I could not confirm a Gulf contractor with benchmark-grade photography. Neri&Hu in Shanghai (https://www.neriandhu.com) documents process of build well and works as the Asian cousin.
- Local independents: small joinery and stone firms with process-photo Instagram accounts are the best everyday standard.

**House style**
- Light: site daylight through openings; shafts of light through dust; work lamps; golden-hour on finished facades. No harsh flash.
- Lens and distance: 35mm for site storytelling, 50-85mm for hands, tools, material close-ups; wide 24mm only for finished spaces with corrected verticals.
- Colour and grade: honest, slightly desaturated; real plaster, timber and steel tones; mild warm bias; keep dust and texture rather than retouching it away.
- People: skilled worker mid-task from behind or profile; helmet only if the site requires it; team in motion, not lined up smiling.
- Props, materials, setting: sawdust, offcuts, tape measure, spirit level, stone slabs, wet plaster; before/during/after at same angle.
- Framing: hands plus tool plus material; layered job-site depth; finished-space hero last.

**Never**
- Hard-hat-and-clipboard handshakes, blueprint stock with a hard hat on top.
- Construction crane at sunset with cheesy lens flare.
- Over-clean site with no dust or mess, models posed as labourers.
- AI-built rooms with impossible joints or beams.
- Hi-vis staged line-up of smiling team facing camera.

**Shot concepts**
- Carpenter planing a timber beam, shavings curling in window light.
- Tile setter pressing a stone tile into adhesive with a trowel.
- Opened wall showing framing and new wiring before closure.
- Painter cutting in along a ceiling with a brush, drop sheet below.
- Kitchen mid-install: cabinet carcasses, a hand levelling a countertop.
- Before/during/after same-angle trio of a bathroom.
- Plasterer smoothing a wall with a trowel, light raking.
- Finished kitchen at golden hour with fresh bread on the island and a resident in soft focus.

**Real only**
- The actual crew, site and finished build.
- Before/after sequences.
- Licence, insurance and safety-compliance imagery.
- Any structural or load-bearing work visualisation.

**House style**
- Light: golden hour and low sun to give panels sheen and long shadows; overcast soft light for installer work; no noon glare.
- Lens and distance: 24-35mm for roof-and-sky scale; 85-200mm compression for panel geometry; macro on cell texture and fittings.
- Colour and grade: deep blue panels versus warm roof tiles; muted sky, controlled highlights; cinematic but natural; avoid neon HDR sky and over-saturated blue.
- People: installer on a roof in harness, mid-task, face partial or in profile; homeowner relaxed, not pointing at a meter.
- Props, materials, setting: mounting rails, microinverters, cable ties, real roof tiles, harness gear, EV charger on a garage wall, battery on a utility wall.
- Framing: strong diagonals along panel rows; wide establishing of home with array; close-up on hands and fittings; leave sky for copy.

**Never**
- Panels in a green field with a sun-and-leaf icon overlay.
- Fake pristine panels on a roof with impossible angles and no mounting hardware (AI tell).
- Installer smiling at camera with thumbs up on a roof without safety gear.
- Wind turbine plus child plus sunflower collage.
- Light-bulb-in-hand, globe-in-hand eco clichés.

**Shot concepts**
- Installer clipping a rail to roof tiles with a drill, sunlight raking the array.

**Real only**

- Safety gear, certifications, tested equipment.
- Any performance or savings graph/readout.

- Note: this industry's photography is mostly weak. Treat the above as best-available, and push for real-people candid style from other home-service references.

**House style**
- Light: soft window daylight in the new home; early-morning light on the truck; no flash. Keep the room bright but not blown.
- Lens and distance: 35mm and 50mm at eye level; wide enough to show the room, tight enough to show the action; macro on tape, labels and handles.
- Colour and grade: warm, natural, kraft-brown cardboard against white walls and wood floor; avoid neon-blue "van" tones.
- People: movers in motion, carrying a sofa or a stacked box, faces partly turned; or a customer sitting on a box with coffee, relaxed.
- Props, materials, setting: cardboard, tape guns, blankets, marker-labelled boxes, a lamp in a bubble wrap sleeve, empty rooms with sun on bare floorboards.
- Framing: doorway thresholds, stacked boxes as geometry, the truck at the curb in street context; vertical crops for social.

**Never**
- Couple kissing among boxes holding a house model or keys.
- Posed movers lined up smiling next to a truck.
- Boxes arranged in perfect pyramids or labelled with lorem-ipsum.
- AI-generated boxes with garbled text, merged hands, floating furniture.
- Miniature toy truck, house-model-on-hand clichés.
